Privacy

Last updated October 25, 2026

Will Dimmit runs curling.dimmit.net and is responsible for what it keeps. For questions, or to see, correct or delete your information, write to will@ultimatetrip.net.

In short, the site keeps what it needs to chart games and to let you keep track of your own. It does not sell anything about anyone. It shows no ads and uses no analytics or tracking services.

What is public

Anyone can see the following, with or without an account:

  • the games, their charts, and what the software read from the video;
  • team names, league names and scores people have typed in;
  • the game list.

Thinking time, which lists teams by name with how long they took per end, is shown only to people signed in.

A chart's link shows that chart to whoever has it. Only the person or team who made it can change it.

Team names are often players' surnames. If one is yours and you would rather it were not on the site, write and it will be taken off.

Search engines are asked not to index the site.

What is kept if you sign in

You sign in with Google, or with a link emailed to you, through Google's Firebase Authentication. The site keeps:

  • Your account: your email address; if you sign in with Google, your Google account's name and profile picture; if you sign in with an emailed link, the name you give (1 to 60 characters, set when you first sign in); when you first and last used the site, and whether your account is validated to name teams or is an admin's.
  • A link opened in a different browser: if you open the emailed link in a different browser or app from the one where you asked for it, the link and a 6-digit code can be used for 10 minutes, so the browser that asked can finish signing you in. They are deleted as soon as the code is used or after 5 wrong tries; any that go unused are removed automatically, usually within a day after the 10 minutes are up. Opening the link is a visit to the site like any other, so its address is also in the request log, kept for 30 days (see Logs).
  • Changes you make, with your account: each team name you set or change on a game (the old name, the new name, and when), and each score you enter. Only the site's operator can see which account made a change.
  • Who can see the list of accounts: the site's operator, and the admins the operator appoints to validate accounts. They see each account's email address, name, and when it first and last used the site.
  • Your shots: which rocks you say you threw, and clips of them. You see these on My shots. Your teammates on the site see them too if you turn sharing on.
  • Your charts and teams: charts you make, edits you make to them, and teams on the site you start or join, with their invites.
  • Practice sessions you start, and clips of their throws.
  • Sensor timings and app keys: if you make a key for a sensor app, its name and when it was made and last used (the key itself is kept only in a form that cannot be read back), and each timing the app sends: the throw's back-line-to-hog and hog-to-hog times and when it crossed the hog line. Timings are kept with the session they were sent into.
  • Flags you send, with your account's email address.

What is kept if you don't sign in

Flags, and edits made through the link of an older chart nobody owns, are kept without a name. To limit abuse, the site keeps a scrambled, one-way code made from your IP address (a salted hash) with charts created and flags sent. The same code is kept in rate-limit counters for two days. The address itself can't be read back from the code.

Logs

Google Cloud, which hosts the site, keeps a standard log of each request for 30 days: the IP address, the browser it came from, the page asked for and the time. The logs are used to keep the site running and to count visitors.

Game video

The site reads games from the club's public YouTube streams, which show the people playing. To read a game, the video is copied to processing computers run by the operator. Most copies are deleted within a few weeks. Some games' video, and still frames taken from games, are kept longer to test and train the software that reads them. Clips are kept in private cloud storage and shared only through links that expire. Nothing on the site identifies anyone from their face.

Practice sessions are read differently. The site reads the club's cameras directly, with the club's agreement, including when nothing is streamed. It records them only from when a player starts a session until, at most, a minute after the session ends: when the player stops it, after 15 minutes without a throw, after three hours, when a league game goes live, or if the session fails. The recording is deleted afterwards, and clips of throws are kept as for games. Outside sessions the cameras are read briefly to keep the site's calibration current, and only measurements are kept, apart from a few still frames of an empty sheet used to fit its layout.

Members who sign in and volunteer to help can be shown still frames and short frame sequences from games, and asked to mark where the stones are or when a thrower let go. What they mark is stored with their account and used to train the software.

Who else handles it

  • Google hosts the site and its data (Cloud Run, Firestore, Cloud Storage, Cloud Logging) and handles sign-in (Firebase Authentication), and sends the sign-in email.
  • YouTube plays the video on a game's page through its own embedded player. YouTube may set its own cookies.
  • Google Fonts serves the site's fonts. Your browser fetches them from Google, which sees your IP address.

Google's handling of all of these is covered by Google's privacy policy. Nothing is shared with anyone else unless the law requires it.

Your browser

The site keeps a few settings in your browser's own storage, such as the league you last picked and where you were in a game. While you sign in with an emailed link, it also keeps the address you typed, a random request number and the page you were on, until you next sign in; it offers them back for about 15 minutes. Staying signed in is kept the same way. There are no advertising cookies.

How long things are kept

  • Your account, and what is tied to it, is kept until you ask for it to be deleted.
  • The record of team-name changes is kept as long as the game is on the site.
  • Rate-limit counters are kept for two days.
  • A link and code held for a link opened in a different browser are deleted as soon as the code is used or after 5 wrong tries; any that go unused are removed automatically, usually within a day after their 10 minutes are up.
  • Request logs are kept for 30 days.

Your choices

Write to will@ultimatetrip.net to ask what is kept about you, to correct it, or to delete your account and what is tied to it. You will get an answer within 30 days. You can turn off sharing your shots with your team at any time on My shots. You can also stop saying which rocks were yours.

Children

Signing in is for people 13 and older. If you learn that a child under 13 has signed in, write and the account will be deleted.

Changes
